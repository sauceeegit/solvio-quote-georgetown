Why 34 panels

Sized to Match a Year, Not a Month.

Your bill gives two consumption figures: 1,699 kWh for this cycle and a 1,901 kWh average over the previous three months. Annualised, that's about 22,816 kWh. One 450 Wp panel in Georgetown produces roughly 686 kWh a year, so 22,816 ÷ 686 ≈ 33.3 panels — rounded up to 34.

Why not more

The PVGIS model already deducts 14% for real-world losses. Adding panels on top for soiling or degradation would count those losses twice. Until twelve months of bills or new loads show higher demand, extra capacity is money spent producing energy you can't yet be paid for.

Why annual, not monthly

This bill covers mid-May to mid-June, a lower-yield stretch of the year. GPL's net-billing programme banks surplus energy as credits for approved systems, so a sunny month can carry a cloudy one. Sizing to the year, rather than the weakest month, avoids paying for an array that over-produces most of the time.
